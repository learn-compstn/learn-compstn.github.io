01 · Abstract

Learning to see through
transformations

Building models robust to rotation, scale, and translation is a core challenge in machine learning and computer vision. Existing approaches often provide only partial, discrete equivariance — or rely on supervision and vast training data to learn equivariant representations.

To achieve fine-grained equivariance from low data, we combine and improve both worlds. Our Learnable Riesz-transform Network (LeaRN) embeds a learnable, steerable frequency-domain weighting that extends built-in group equivariance to include continuous rotation and scale. We pair it with CompSTN, a cascade of Spatial Transformer Networks that sequentially estimates composite transformations, slashing the combinatorial data requirement.

We provide theoretical generalization guarantees through tighter PAC-Bayesian bounds, propose a new unsupervised rotation consistency metric (RoE), and demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art across unsupervised object discovery benchmarks — with the largest gains under limited data.

02 · Method

Two core contributions

LeaRN and CompSTN work in tandem: the first instills built-in spectral equivariance, the second breaks composite transformation estimation into simpler sequential steps.

📡

Learnable Riesz Network

Extends the Riesz transform — a steerable, scale-equivariant frequency-domain operator — with learnable radial weights parameterized as a sparse mixture of Laplace distributions:

w(ξ) = Σₖ wₖ·exp(−|‖ξ‖−μₖ|)
ℱ(LR(I))(ξ) = w(ξ)·ℱ(R(I))(ξ)

Provides continuous SE(2) equivariance, mitigates aliasing, and is proven to tighten PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds.

🔗

Composite Spatial Transformers

A cascade of two STN modules that decomposes composite transformation estimation into a sequence of simpler problems:

  1. STN₁: Estimates and corrects translation & scale using rotation-invariant features
  2. STN₂: Estimates rotation on the already-canonicalized glimpse

Reduces data need from O(|S|·|α|·|t|) to O(|S|·|t| + |α|). Exponentially fewer examples needed.

📐

Rotation-Offset Entropy

A new metric for unsupervised rotation recovery. Since no reference angle exists without labels, we measure the consistency of predicted offsets via normalized Shannon entropy:

RoE = H(p) / log₂(n)
0 = perfect · 1 = pure disorder

First metric to quantify rotation consistency in unsupervised settings. Our model achieves RoE ≈ 0.35 vs. no measurement for prior work.

Why sequential decomposition matters

Standard CNNs must see every combination of rotation, scale, and translation for each object — a combinatorial explosion. LeaRN handles scale and translation by design; CompSTN's two-step decomposition means the rotation STN only needs to learn rotation after translation and scale are already corrected. The result is an exponential reduction in required training examples.

04 · Results

State-of-the-art across all benchmarks

Evaluated on MTRS-MNIST, MVTec D2S, MVTec Screws, and MQTT-MNIST for object discovery, and on SR-MNIST, Tomtwin, Plankton, and MVTec for autoencoding — under both Less Data and More Data regimes. LeaRN-CompSTN consistently achieves the best AP, mAP, NMI, ARI, and SSIM, and is the only method to report rotation consistency (RoE).
